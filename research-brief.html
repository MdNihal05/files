School SaaS — Research

draft — pending team review, not pushed / not final

TL;DR — what the research says

  • The Indian school-ERP market has 60+ vendors already selling attendance/fees/exams/role-based access — the original feature list is table stakes, not a pitch.
  • The buyer (principal/trustee) and the daily user (teacher) are different people with different needs — most rollouts fail because teachers, not admins, quietly stop using the tool.
  • Animated lectures, AI doubt-chatbots and auto-generated tests are already mainstream (Extramarks, Teachmint, SPYRAL) — not a moat, a different and better-funded fight.
  • Chosen wedge: teacher-adoption-first, modeled on ClassDojo's bottom-up playbook — win the teacher before selling the school.
  • Nothing above is validated with a real person yet. That's the next step, not the pitch deck.

What the actual buyer wants to see

Principals, trustees and finance heads hold the budget — not teachers, not parents. They buy dashboards that answer: which classes have the worst fee-default rate, when attendance is starting to drop, which admission channel is actually working, and — for a trust running multiple schools — one consolidated view instead of per-school reports stitched together by hand.

02 The adoption graveyard

More useful than any feature list — this is every way a school has already tried a tool like this and quietly stopped using it.

partnership, not productSmall schools (<~600 students) need hand-holding and phone/WhatsApp support, 2–3 modules to start — not a full suite dropped on day one.
unpaid extra workTeachers see data entry as work with zero personal benefit → they stop, admin can't force compliance.
nothing to checkParent apps get uninstalled when updates are rare — attendance once a day, fees once a month isn't sticky.
English-onlyLanguage — apps without local-language support fail in tier-2/3 towns.
results dayServers crash on result day / fee due-day — a scaling cost nobody budgets for until it happens.
paper, just in caseSchools keep paper backups in parallel — trust in "cloud only" isn't there yet.
reported directlyVendor complaints: slow support response, breakage during updates, confusing linked workflows.

03 Product architecture — three planes

How the platform is structured underneath, in plain terms — one deployment serves every school, every school-chain, and us as the operator, without any of them seeing each other's data.

super-admin (us) > group / trust (a school chain, easy to miss in most competitors) > individual school (day-to-day use)

"Model routing & budgets" = controlling which AI model handles which task and what it costs us per school, centrally. "k-anonymised cross-tenant benchmarks" = comparing schools to each other in aggregate without ever exposing one school's raw data to another.

05 Migration

Every incumbent's switching cost is the same: a school's data is already trapped in Excel sheets, a previous ERP export, or a WhatsApp group history. Part of the offer is doing that migration for them — not handing them an importer and a spreadsheet template. This directly targets the "schools keep paper/old systems in parallel" failure mode in the graveyard above: if switching costs them nothing but time on our side, the trust-in-cloud objection weakens.

06 Proposed differentiators — reality-checked

Animated lectures (incl. lab experiments), automated tests, and an AI academic-assistant chatbot — requested for inclusion, checked against the market before calling them a strength.

These are already mainstream, not a gap: Extramarks ships "ALEX," an AI studybot that solves doubts instantly, plus AI lesson planning and interactive 3D lab-style video. Teachmint has an AI-powered digital board with a "Smart Homework Creator." Dedicated players (e.g. SPYRAL) sell AI lab simulations mapped to CBSE/ICSE with auto-generated quizzes. One report puts 68% of Indian schools already using some AI teaching tool, commonly stacking 3–5 tools rather than one platform.

Building these doesn't create distance from the ERP incumbents — it moves the competitive set to better-funded content/AI players (Extramarks-class), a different and more capital-intensive fight than the lightweight adoption wedge below. Worth deciding on purpose:

Option A

Adoption-first now (Section 07's wedge). Add content/AI once there's a paying base. Smaller bet, competes mainly with ERP vendors, not content studios.

Option B

Content/AI from day one. Bigger bet — competitive set now includes Extramarks, Teachmint-AI, SPYRAL. Changes funding and build-time assumptions considerably.

One piece does support the adoption wedge directly either way: automated tests (auto-grading, question-bank generation) saves a teacher time on something they already resent — a concrete week-one hook. Animated lectures and a student-facing chatbot serve a different audience (students) and a different motive (content quality) — not the teacher-adoption problem itself.

07 The wedge: win the teacher first

Modeled on ClassDojo: it did not sell to administrators first. One teacher, a free tool (behavior management + parent updates), reached 35,000 classrooms in 12 weeks by word of mouth, built by listening to ~20,000 teachers directly — eventually ~90% of US schools, bottom-up. Its first version solved something small and immediately gratifying for a teacher in week one, not a full admin-system replacement.

That's the shape of the bet: build something a teacher personally wants to keep using without being told to, and let adoption spread the way ClassDojo's did — instead of selling a mandate to a principal that teachers then quietly resent and abandon.

09 Validation plan

Two interview guides — will teachers actually adopt it voluntarily, and will the budget holder tolerate/pay for a tool that entered the school bottom-up.

Teacher interview guide 8 questions

Screening: school type (private, board — CBSE/ICSE/state), size, grades taught, years teaching, current digital tools in use.

current state
  1. Walk me through your day yesterday, end to end — where did time go that wasn't teaching?
  2. What's the most annoying repetitive task you do for school administration (not teaching itself)?
  3. Do you currently use any app/software for attendance, marks, or parent messages? Walk me through actually using it, right now if possible.
  4. What do you do in parallel with that (paper register, personal WhatsApp, Excel)? Why both?
pain validation
  1. Has your school ever made you switch to a new software/app? What happened — did you keep using it 3 months later? Why or why not?
  2. If a tool saved you 30 min/day, would you actually change your habit, or is habit-switching itself the cost regardless of time saved?
  3. Who benefits when you enter attendance/marks into a system — you, the school, or the parent? Does that change how much effort you put in?
wedge-specific / willingness
  1. What would a tool have to do in the first week for you to keep using it without being told to?
Principal / trustee interview guide 8 questions

Screening: role (principal/trustee/finance head), school size, board, current ERP status (none / Excel+WhatsApp / named vendor), years in that decision seat.

current state
  1. How do you currently get attendance, fee-default, and exam data — who compiles it, how often, how do you trust it's accurate?
  2. Have you evaluated or bought school management software before? What happened — still using it, switched away, or never got past the pilot?
  3. If you did adopt something and it didn't stick, what actually broke — the software, teachers not using it, cost, or something else?
pain validation
  1. What's the one report or number you wish you could see instantly but currently can't (or takes too long to compile)?
  2. What did teachers say when you rolled out your last new tool — resistance, indifference, adoption? Did you have to enforce it?
wedge-specific — the actual test
  1. If a tool got teachers using it voluntarily first — free, useful to them personally, no rollout mandate needed — would that make you more or less likely to trust/adopt it school-wide? Why?
  2. Would you pay for a tool your teachers already use and like, even if it started as "shadow IT" you didn't choose?
willingness signal
  1. What would you need to see (data, a pilot, a reference school) before you'd sign a contract?
